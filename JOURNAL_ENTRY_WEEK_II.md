Today we received the project Soundscape Explorer project, which was not our first collective choice but was in our top 3, which we were happy about since it aligned to a lot of team interests such as 3D modelling and programming.

Elios did a great presentation on Design Thinking Framework, and although very interesting, I found it hard to take notes on because the pace was quite fast, but still, my main takeaway was the importance of visualizing the project through different lenses. Them being
The ideation: what the client wants, desires to see in the final project. What they envisioned as a final solution.
Technology: Meaning what are the technology limitations. What is available and what can be acquired.
Business: What are the organization limitations and resources. What can be provided and what to be outsourced.

Although there are many other concepts of the lecture that not only are interested but, **need** to be applied, the one of the lenses stuck with me because of the complexity of our project.
I reminded the group that more important than having an mediocre **product**, it is better to have a more concrete and well finished **prototype**. That being said we need a really good proof of concept, say for a floor or even a room rather than the whole building.

Since the project brief brings up different elements that our client wanted such as a *map view*, *360-degree view*, *immersive audio* and *points of interest*, it raises more questions than ideas because although these technologies are related, all of them together can create an overwhelming experience. So we started formulating the questions for Florian, to better understand his view for this project.

In terms of technology, since this project brief brings up different kinds of interaction, we need to consider what we acquire ourselves or borrow from labs and the CDA, for example, a 360 camera to capture images or a special microphone setup to process imersive audio.

Finally, from a business point of view, we need to understand what our client can also provide. Be in terms of tools but also materials, assets, projects that he developed in the past or even building blueprints that we can use.

Given the project brief, the presentation and the fact that our heads possessed more question marks than ideas, the parts of the framework we mostly adopted were those that would help us organize these questions in order to as Florian the right questions in the next week. That being said, even though we sketched a few ideas and researched concepts, we did not brainstorm a lot. Instead we took the time to start a channel on Discord, start a board on Fizzy and concentrate each aspect of the project in a different place. This was the way we found to streamline the ideas and questions to come.
