This week we finally met with our client, Professor Florian Grond, which helped us clarify many of the questions we had after receiving the project brief. It was especially useful to hear more about the project from his perspective and understand what he is actually hoping to achieve, rather than only interpreting the brief ourselves.

One of the main things we learned is that Florian already has a lot of the equipment and material that could be useful for the project. He has immersive speakers, different audio recording devices, and recordings that we could potentially use as a starting point. He also talked about some of his previous research involving tactile pads and how he is interested in creating ways for people to experience or get a preview of environments that they might not normally experience in the same way.

This also helped us think more about the target audience. We initially talked mostly about the general public, but Florian mentioned researchers and people with disabilities as important potential users. This changes the way we need to think about the experience, especially in terms of accessibility and what information the user should actually be able to get from it.

The map is still one of the main elements of the project. The idea is that recordings could be attached to specific locations on a map, allowing the user to explore an environment through its sounds. We also started discussing the possibility of recording places ourselves, such as a metro station, and using these recordings as part of our prototype. One idea that came up was almost like a street view, but with the audio being much more important and “pimped up” compared to a normal Google Street View experience.

After the meeting, we had a better idea of what we need to research before deciding exactly what the final experience should be. We want to explore recording applications and ways of capturing spatial audio, as well as how immersive audio channels can be played back. We also need to research Eclipsa Audio and see how it could fit into the project. On the visual side, we need to look at how we could create the map view and eventually connect it to a 3D or VR environment.

For our milestones, we are therefore starting with research rather than immediately trying to build the whole experience. Our first concrete goals are to understand the recording and playback technologies, create a basic map view, and experiment with inserting recordings into it. The meeting gave us a much clearer direction, and instead of having mostly questions like last week, we now have a better idea of what we need to test to see what is actually possible.
